The jam hangar findings. The theme exists to solve the "blank canvas" problem — it gives thousands of jammers a shared creative constraint so they can start immediately. Theme use is NOT required by the rules — it is a recommendation, and since LD50 participants can opt out of the Theme rating category. BUT if you stay opted in, raters judge the fit. Theme is one of seven official rating categories: Fun, Innovation, Theme, Graphics, Audio, Humor, Mood. Community members rate entries during a two-to-three-week voting window; no panel of judges. The core tension for our deploy-ready hangar: the hangar is the game's proven core — it must NOT be redesigned at six PM Friday. Theme integration must therefore be a content, framing, and narrative layer over a stable core, not a mechanics rewrite. Raters score what they can perceive: "there should be a clear link between your game and the theme, so an untrained player can encounter the game cold." A theme only visible in the devlog does not score.

Validation — THEME_HOOK execution specifics. One: verb matching, the engineer's technique — brainstorm the theme's VERBS, then route those verbs through the hook into existing mechanics and labels. Koster's theory of fun underpins this: theme works when the player experiences the same verbs in mechanics. Two: three matching layers the hook can drive without touching core sim — narrative matching, a one-paragraph story with hangar and combat labels renamed to the theme's verbs; aesthetic matching, adjectives of the theme guiding UI tint, pilot barks, mission briefings; tension and stakes, theme as conflict between opposing aspects, Sid Meier's "interesting choices." Three: unexpected angle beats literal read — "Just look at theme from angle no one would." Examples: "Assemble" became blind chemical-element assembly; "Odd One Out" became spot-the-AI chat messages; "Defense plus time" became cybersecurity. Metaphorical interpretations earn higher Innovation scores. Four: timebox the interpretation — hard cap two to three hours Friday night; brainstorm ten to twenty ideas; your second or third idea beats your first, because the first is what everyone else had. Five: winners' pattern — "a single game mechanic directly tied to the theme" plus "polished core loop rather than a half-finished feature list," completable in five to ten minutes. Fun and Theme are the two categories winners prioritize. Six: theme is a spectrum — the more theme you apply, the more thematic it is. A fully-decorative theme sits at the low end; mechanics-expressed at the high end. Ship at least one mechanics-expressed beat. Seven: the theme-versus-mechanic trap — words like "cooperation" are gameplay mechanics in disguise, not themes.

The techniques — last-mile polish from the gamestack checklist. Order matters: control, then simulation, then forgiveness, then juice. Never juice a verb that doesn't feel right yet. Latency budget: under fifty milliseconds input-to-pixel at sixty fps. Input forgiveness: buffer inputs one-hundred to two-hundred milliseconds; coyote time four to six frames. Hit-stop: per-event table, three to ten frames scaled to magnitude, with a cumulative cap — matching our existing rate cap. Screen shake: trauma-based, which our three-state intensity already fits. Particles: distinct signature per event, cap concurrent emitters — our prewarmed pool is the right tool. Ease every value change — no linear interpolation. UI juice: hover at one-hundred to two-hundred milliseconds, press squish under one-hundred, audio on every action, distinct clicks per type, no silent menus or instant state cuts. Feedback budget: aim Medium to High band; Extreme juice hurts as much as None; keep a rarity ladder. Onboarding is the number-one silent killer: "most close the tab after thirty seconds if the player can't figure out the controls." Missing audio actively hurts the Audio rating. Deployment pipeline BEFORE day one.

What to avoid. One: the decorative-theme trap — theme as only names and colors; raters read it instantly. Two: redesigning the core at six PM Friday. Three: scope creep dressed as theme. Four: overpromising on the submission page. Five: the mechanic masquerading as theme. Six: the obvious first idea. Seven: untested browser builds — test the itch web build on iPhone Safari AND desktop Chrome before the deadline. Eight: theme tunnel vision at the cost of Fun — winners put Fun AND Theme first.
