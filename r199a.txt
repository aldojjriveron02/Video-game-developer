The jam perf findings. Web-Standards.dev field guide: keep EVERY frame under ten milliseconds, not just sixteen-point-seven — the stricter internal budget leaves headroom, because anything exceeding frame time reads as lag. Fixed timestep with render interpolation keeps logic consistent across hardware. NixieFX: sixteen-point-seven milliseconds is for EVERYTHING, including the browser's compositing overhead, not just game code. Their audit order when stutters appear: bound work to the visible window, then culling, then per-frame allocation, then pool prewarming, then lists rebuilt per frame, then render passes measured separately. Rule: profile on a real device — "don't shrink the feature to fake the numbers." Steve Losh, LD34 postmortem — solo dev, seventy-two hours, vanilla JS, zero budget. His perf line: "During the last day I spent an hour or so using a profiler to identify the hottest spots." Also: a jam is not the time for new tech, and "releasing before the end" — test-deploy before the deadline, because perf problems are discovered at deploy, not in dev. Validation. VolumeShaderTest's iPhone GPU protocol — the only mobile-thermal protocol found: phones throttle GPU clocks after about thirty seconds of sustained load. Their rules: device fully cooled before the recorded run, Low Power Mode OFF, compare only the same mode against itself. Reference baseline on iPhone 15 Pro Safari: sixty average FPS, P10 fifty-six, ninety-three percent stability. Warning: ignore benchmark sites that don't standardize cool-down. Chrome DevTools: the Performance panel records frame-by-frame with orange and red flags on jank; the FPS meter gives a live estimate.
