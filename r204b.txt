Part 2 of 3. Twenty minutes of custom CSS takes a page from prototype to professional. Validation — Bromley's protocol, the professional basis for our one-shot. Two test types: comprehension — do players understand — and appeal — are they gripped. Recruit from your competitor set; validate narrative interest by having candidates verbally describe story beats that stuck with them from comparable games. Comprehension: one-to-one moderated, slide per story beat, three open questions per beat — "What is happening currently? Why is this happening? What do you expect to happen next?" Never just "is this clear?" Test early at storyboard stage, recheck at milestones. Appeal: regularly ask "how interested are you in what happens next," chart drop-off over time; define the intended emotion per beat and compare against players' word-list picks. The traps: players experience a story as intended only the FIRST time — never reuse playtesters, screen for prior exposure; screen out friends, family, and industry people; do NOT ask for story ideas or "do you like it" — "a recipe for generic low-impact storytelling"; breaks change recall — follow up weeks later on what they remember. The techniques — Vois on barks that don't grate. A bark is dialogue that survives repetition — "Can't do that" is useful the first time, a personal insult by the end of a puzzle. Separate informational barks from personality lines — warnings must stay legible during combat. For each trigger note frequency, urgency, possible overlap, whether another sound already exists; mark interruptible triggers. Variant counts: three for routine triggers, six for common — a starting exercise. Vary construction — "On my way," "Moving," "I'll get there." More lines don't fix over-triggering — fix cooldowns and priority. Put actionable info first; cut the rest. Set a duration budget per trigger. Test the line while doing the action. Define the character's normal energy plus allowed range; give every line a specific intention — reassure an ally, acknowledge a task, warn the squad. Compare the set side by side — if every variant ends with an emphatic flourish, the pool feels repetitive.
