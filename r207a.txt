LD rules: seventy-two hours, teams welcome, Audio is a judged category — one to five stars on "how good the game sounds, or how effective the sound design is." Submission Hour after the deadline, plus a post-jam window for bug fixes — no new features, changes flagged in a changelog. The asset-origin rule: third-party or pre-made audio is allowed, but you must OPT OUT of the Audio voting category and have the legal right. Our WebAudio synth engine is a tool — like using a game engine — fine; but any pre-composed music or SFX dropped in as-is during LD60 should be re-created in-window via jsfxr or BeepBox to stay eligible. And the fun fact with teeth: sfxr, "the popular sound effect generator," was originally created FOR Ludum Dare — procedural synth SFX is culturally native to this jam. Validation — the mix-check protocol. Loudness targets from multiple twenty-twenty-five and twenty-twenty-six sources converge: minus twenty-three to minus twenty-four LUFS integrated, true peak at minus one to minus two dBTP; minus fourteen LUFS for YouTube trailers only. Measurement: ffmpeg's ebur128 filter — integrated LUFS, loudness range, true peak per EBU R128 — free, scriptable, the exact shape for a validation gate. Bugnet's indie audio QA taxonomy: incorrect audio, missing audio, clipping and distortion, volume balance, timing and delay, platform-specific audio. Protocol: test on real devices — emulators miss device-specific bugs — structured checklists with expected versus actual, severity-ranked bug bashes, re-test after every fix. A Sound Effect's mix discipline: mix for readability — the player must always parse gameplay-critical sounds; pre-dub and pre-mix passes per category before a final full-game pass; voice-limiting and prioritization at the emitter level; mix against the diversity of endpoints; schedule dedicated mixing passes as a final milestone.
