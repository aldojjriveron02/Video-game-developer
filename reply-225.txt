The jam perf findings. Perf "freeze" for a jam means two things locked simultaneously: the frame budget verified and held on the SHIP build, and no code changes that can move perf numbers allowed after a cutoff. Game Developer's LD submission advice: target completion about two hours before the deadline and build and ship early and often — upload a warmup game first so submission mechanics are rehearsed. The Generalist Programmer's game-jam guide recommends a feature-freeze deadline typically twelve to eighteen hours before submission: after it, no new features — only bug fixes and polish. For our slice the perf freeze should be stricter than the feature freeze: nothing that changes draw calls, allocations, or the audio graph after the perf lock. And the cautionary tale — Vampire Survivors shipped in Phaser 3 as browser tech, then migrated to Unity in twenty-twenty-two to improve performance as scale exploded. Web tech ships hits, but the perf ceiling is real — know it BEFORE the jam, not during ratings.

Validation — the final perf checklist with numbers. The web.dev RAIL model: at sixty fps the absolute frame ceiling is sixteen-point-seven milliseconds, but browsers need about six to render, so the guideline is to produce each frame in ten milliseconds or less. At one-twenty hertz the ceiling is eight-point-three — the number our part-ten audit must be re-measured against on the final build. Input response: RAIL says respond within one hundred milliseconds, budget fifty for actual input handling. Tooling for the final pass: Chrome DevTools CPU throttling, FPS meter, Performance Monitor for CPU, heap, DOM nodes, layouts per second. And the critical rule from nixiefx's mobile performance guide: profile on a REAL device via chrome inspect or Safari's Develop menu, and "never shrink features to fake the numbers" — re-measure the real game, don't stub systems out. The console-grade analog from Game Programming Patterns' Object Pool chapter: console manufacturers require soak tests — running the game unattended in demo mode for extended periods. Our twenty-minute thermal soak is this game's equivalent; the freeze checklist should require the soak on the FINAL slice build, on the iPhone itself, since sustained load triggers iOS thermal throttling that short benchmarks miss. WebAudio: MDN best practices — one AudioContext per page, resume on user gesture; freeze checklist must re-verify unlock plus ducking plus the locked mix on iOS Safari, not just desktop.

The techniques — freeze-safe perf polish, nothing that restructures code. Fixed-size, prewarmed, bounded pools with a free list — free list gives O(1) alloc and free; freeze-safe validation means asserting the pool never grows past cap under M3 VEX duel load. Offscreen pre-render of repeating primitives: render static grid and arena elements once to an offscreen canvas, blit per frame. Integer coordinates — Math dot floor on drawImage, never scale inside drawImage, cache pre-scaled sizes. alpha false on getContext when no transparency is needed. Batch canvas calls, minimize state changes, avoid shadowBlur and per-frame text rendering. DPR cap around two and draw-call ceiling in the low hundreds as measured targets. GC discipline: near-zero steady-state allocation in the hot loop; split hot and cold paths. Timestep guards from Glenn Fiedler's "Fix Your Timestep": clamp frameTime — his example clamps at zero-point-two-five seconds — keep fixed-dt simulation with an accumulator so behavior is frame-rate independent, and leave headroom so the sim can catch up from spikes.

What to avoid — the perf freeze traps. The spiral of death: variable timesteps without a max-step clamp — when the sim can't keep up, catch-up steps pile up and it falls further behind; never enter the jam with unclamped delta time; a background tab or thermal dip during a long rating session will trigger it. Optimizing without profiling — late "optimization" commits that touch the renderer during freeze are the classic jam perf regression source. Last-minute render-target changes: DPR bumps, enabling shadows or transparency, or new full-screen effects after lock. Feature work in the perf window. Shipping the perf HUD at full cost — the HUD itself consumes frame budget; JAM_BUILD should keep it dormant. Audio graph growth under freeze: adding voices or effect nodes per event instead of reusing nodes. Judder on one-twenty hertz from remainder time in the accumulator — if the loop shows stutter only at one-twenty, that's the first suspect.
