Part 2 of 3. The techniques — last-mile polish. Master limiter ceiling at minus one dBTP — a safety net, not a mixer. Ducking: dip BGM under pilot barks and hit-stop windows so voice and SFX stay readable. Voice management: our cap of twelve is in the right zone; add importance prioritization — barks over UI over enemy SFX over ambient — and emitter-level limiting so the cap steals the least important first. Hit-stop plus audio: our zero-point-three-five per second cap already bounds SFX bursts; pair loud impacts with the freeze and duck BGM in that window for feel without loudness spikes. The iOS silent-switch fix: navigator dot audioSession dot type equals playback, Safari sixteen-point-four plus, feature-detectable. WebKit's fourth AudioContext state — interrupted: a frozen context accepts scheduled tones but currentTime stops advancing, so queued audio bursts on resume. Resume logic must check for interrupted explicitly. Autoplay: the context starts suspended; it resumes only after a user gesture AND start on a source node. What to avoid. One: iOS silent switch silently muting Web Audio — no error, state stays running — while audio and video elements still play. With our iPhone 17 target, this is the number-one trap. Two: the playback fix has costs — output-only, incompatible with getUserMedia, interrupts the user's music instead of mixing. Three: the interrupted burst — queued tones pile up and fire all at once, sounds like a bug. Four: suspended at load — judges who hear silence in the first ten seconds mark Audio down.
