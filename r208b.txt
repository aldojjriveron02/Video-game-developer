Part 2 of 3. The thermal protocol: a phone can hold sixty fps for three to five minutes then start throttling, so test sustained ten-to-thirty-minute sessions, not thirty-second bursts. Target lower baseline than peak. If frame time creeps over fifteen to twenty minutes, the build fails a real session even if the first sixty seconds look perfect. The techniques — last-mile polish. MDN's optimizing canvas: pre-render repeated objects on offscreen canvases; integer coordinates; never scale in drawImage — cache pre-scaled copies; layered canvases for static versus dynamic; context with alpha false when opaque; batch canvas calls; avoid shadowBlur; rAF; dirty regions; match canvas pixels to CSS size. NixieFX: zero steady-state allocation after warmup; avoid map, filter, forEach in hot loops; no object or array literals inside loops; helpers mutate caller-owned outputs; ring buffers over Array dot shift; pooled events; cap DPR at two — "single biggest win on high-DPR phones"; keep draw calls in the low hundreds; cull offscreen. GeneralistProgrammer: profile, fix, re-profile until budget met; diagnose CPU versus GPU by dropping resolution fifty percent; pooling up to sixty times faster with zero GC; dynamic resolution gives thirty to fifty percent GPU headroom; sixty fps is sixteen-point-six-seven milliseconds; mobile thirty fps is thirty-three-point-three. Arcadigo's traps: iOS Low Power Mode throttles GPU heavily — "judges playing on a throttled iPhone will see a slideshow regardless of your code"; in-app browsers perform worse than real Safari; never profile with DevTools open — it skews results. What to avoid. Single-source measurement. Shrinking the feature to make a bad number look good instead of diagnosing. Unpooled steady-state allocation — GC stutters.
