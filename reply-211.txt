The jam tutorial findings. Thompson's rater problem: LD games are rated on Overall, Innovation, Fun, Theme, Graphics, Audio, Humor — raters decide fast, and a tutorial that delays "the fun" directly costs Fun and Overall points. His rules: submit a warmup prototype before the jam so the pipeline is proven — we satisfy this; set a personal deadline two hours before the real one because submission hour is when things go most wrong; don't ship missing categories — his LD28 had no audio, automatic N/A. George Fan's attention-budget problem from his GDC twenty-twelve talk: players arrive with a FINITE willingness to learn; it grows only as they play. His two rules: "Use Fewer Words" and "Just get the player to do it once." A jam tutorial must spend that budget only on what is unique. Hedges' player interviews: tutorials players LIKE don't feel like tutorials, don't disrupt flow, have low fail chance, include a tutorial boss, are skippable, allow experimentation, use short messages, focus on unique mechanics. Dark Souls' Undead Asylum is the model: early failure nearly impossible, free experimentation, then a tutorial boss. Mega Man X's first level taught everything with no tutorial section at all.

Validation — Kleenex execution specifics. Eastshade's tissue testing: watch someone who has NEVER played, with ZERO explaining or guiding — "the more unfamiliar they are with your game and the less you speak while they play it, the better." Called tissue because a player can only be first-time once — be frugal with fresh eyes. One to two testers per session generated a month of fixes. Two hard-won specifics: IN-PERSON beats remote — without seeing the exact moments, feedback is uninterpretable; and add diegetic breadcrumbs — in-world hints that don't spell things out — "there can never be too many breadcrumbs." Nielsen's five-user math: model N times one minus one-minus-L to the n, typical L thirty-one percent; the curve bends at five users, after which "you are wasting your time by observing the same findings repeatedly." Better: THREE studies of five users than one study of fifteen. The second five-user round catches most of the remaining fifteen percent AND validates the fixes. This is the quantitative license for our gate — and it mandates a re-test round after fixes, not a single pass.

The techniques — last-mile polish. The eight-word rule, sourced: George Fan's rule was NO MORE THAN EIGHT WORDS PER SENTENCE — via Hedges. Audit every tutorial text box against it. Pine's five invisible-tutorial methods: AFFORDANCES — design communicates use; SKILL GATES — block progress until the taught action is performed once, like Celeste's prologue freezing time until you dash; DEMONSTRATIVE — show don't tell, like Half-Life 2's barnacle eating a crow; TRIAL AND ERROR — safe failure with crystal-clear causality, like Spelunky; CONTEXT-SENSITIVE HINTS — Hollow Knight shows a hint only if the player stalls about five SECONDS at a skill gate. Gee's principle: "Information on Demand and Just in Time" — deliver each instruction at the moment of need, never before.

What to avoid. Hedges' thirteen negative patterns: hand-holding, not skippable, patronizing tone, forced videos, information overload, ruins flow, irrelevant info, FAILED to provide necessary info, constant pauses, too long, unskippable popups, forced pace, repetitive. The text-wall trap: adding MORE tutorial text to fix confusion — fix the affordance or skill gate instead. The remote-only trap. The one-big-study trap.

Shipped examples. BABA IS YOU — Nordic Game Jam twenty-seventeen: the rules ARE the tutorial, opening levels teach by doing, no separate tutorial mode. Skill gates plus trial-and-error with perfect causality. KEEP TALKING AND NOBODY EXPLODES — Global Game Jam twenty-fourteen: the tutorial is EXTERNALIZED in the printed manual; co-op asymmetry does the teaching. SUPERHOT — seven-day FPS challenge: teaches "time moves when you move" with minimal diegetic one-liners, pure Fan-style.

If the player stalls five seconds at a skill gate, the coach target escalates to urgent: orange, faster pulse, plus telemetry. Otherwise it stays silent. The timer clears when the step completes.
