The four-device gate: one, iPhone seventeen Safari with silent switch OFF; two, iPhone seventeen Safari with silent switch ON; three, desktop Chrome at one-hundred percent volume; four, desktop Safari or Android Chrome. Per device: audio unlocks on first tap; no clipping at max volume during the densest second; BGM ducking audible; no missing or double SFX; barks intelligible on tiny speakers. Cheap earbuds as a fifth informal check. The techniques — last-mile polish. Master chain order: per-bus gain nodes — SFX, BGM, bark-voice, UI — into a master gain, into a DynamicsCompressor as safety limiter, into destination. Standard limiter posture: threshold around minus-six dB, fast attack about three milliseconds, moderate release about a quarter second. Ducking: on bark start, BGM bus dips about minus-six dB via setTargetAtTime, restores with a point-three-second time constant after the bark ends — already in our Vois table; keep dip and restore both exponential, never linear to zero. Click-free one-shots: five-to-twenty-millisecond fade envelopes on every synthesized voice; seamless BGM loops need exact loop points or equal-power crossfades. Voice management: cap concurrent voices, steal oldest or quietest first; pre-decode and pre-generate all buffers before gameplay; prewarm the audio voice pool exactly like the particle pool. iOS unlock done right: call AudioContext dot resume synchronously inside the touch handler — if resume runs after an async gap instead of synchronously inside the gesture, iOS may keep the context suspended. What to avoid. One: autoplay before gesture — Chrome's MEI policy and Safari's suspended-context rule mean any sound scheduled before a gesture is silently dropped. Two: the iOS silent-ring switch mutes Web Audio output — the number-one "no sound on iPhone" jam trap, and our target device is iPhone seventeen. The wild workaround: playing a silent HTML5 audio element to change the audio session category.
