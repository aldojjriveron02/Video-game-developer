The techniques — freeze-safe polish, no new logic, data-only. One: the one-sentence onboarding, Celeste Classic pattern — the shipped jam game's entire itch description is "A PICO-8 platformer about climbing a mountain, made in four days. Use the arrow keys, X, and C." Goal plus controls in one breath. For our duel entry: one line of eight words or under, then silence. Two: the five invisible-tutorial methods — affordances with pulse and glow on legal tiles, skill gates with a safe first-turn arena, demonstrative method where VEX and pilot barks SHOW the mechanic, trial and error only where failure is free, context-sensitive hints via the five-second stall. Three: reverse-engineered teaching levels, the Baba Is You pattern — creator Teikari implements a new word then reverse-engineers a level requiring that interaction; jam application: the M3 arena layout itself forces exactly one new decision per turn — the map IS the tutorial. Four: freeze-safe equals data-only polish — after freeze, the only permitted tutorial changes are hint-string wording, hint timing, and highlight visuals; the tutorial runs through one method and flag, mirroring the THEME_HOOK discipline, so nothing in tutorial logic can break during the seventy-two hours.

What to avoid. One: frontloaded text walls — the number-one antipattern; the freeze trap is "just one more explanatory line" added Friday night. Two: trial-and-error WITH punishment — never let VEX one-shot the player's mech during the teaching turn. Three: teaching known controls — on touch-first iPhone, do not tutorialize tap and swipe; teach only game verbs. Four: tutorial coupled to volatile systems — any hint reading jam-week-changed state like theme adapters breaks silently at freeze. Five: late copy churn — changing hint strings invalidates the Kleenex Friday wording and risks eight-word violations. Six: self-testing only — the dev already knows the game; design for the player's mental model.
