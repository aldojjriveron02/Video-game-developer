Validation — the agnostic protocol specifics. Nielsen: test five users per qualitative study; better to distribute budget across more studies than more users per study — "ROI drops like a stone with a bigger N"; for low-overhead agile, "often optimal to test as few as two users per study." NNGroup's cheat sheet: "Avoid influencing participants during testing" — facilitators too often jump in. Shafer's blind protocol: run unguided; observation is the key data source; have players think aloud; do NOT coach, explain, or reword rules; note where they stall — that is the finding. Schade on leading questions: the interviewer is "the authority in the room" — participants confirm what they think you want. Neutral only: "What are you trying to do?" and "What just happened?" — never "Did the hit-stop feel good?" The execution shape: two rounds of five blind testers on the M3 duel, iPhone-class touch plus desktop, silent observation plus think-aloud, one task — "beat the VEX duel" — success equals completes unassisted; log every stall over five seconds, reusing our existing stall-hint timer as the threshold.
