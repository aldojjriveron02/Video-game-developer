The official LD rules: the Jam is seventy-two hours, teams and individuals, base code and third-party assets allowed — and note, our WebAudio synth is original, so Audio stays contestable, no opt-out. The rating criteria include Humor — "humorous dialog, jokes, and puns" — and Mood — "storytelling, emotion, and the vibe you get while playing." Mood IS the narrative score. Theme is scored separately as a social rule. There's a one-hour Submission Hour after the deadline, then it closes. "The ideal Ludum Dare game can be played right in the browser, in a few minutes, alone." And the Coolness system: playing and rating others raises your game's discoverability. Thompson: work with tools you know; build a warm-up prototype that's submittable BEFORE the jam; his LD28 entry had NO audio — automatic N/A, one-star, a significant overall hit. Brainstorm about two hours at reveal. One unique focus, don't overscope. Finish two hours before the deadline — "the hour where things go most wrong is during submission hour." In those two hours: icon, screenshots, gameplay video. After: rate at least ten games a day, postmortem. Arcweave's forty-eight-hour narrative workflow from a real seventy-two-hour jam with a hundred-twenty participants: the ten-minute rule — "If you can't explain your story in ten minutes, it's too complex for a jam." Three moments, not three acts: a hook that grabs, a choice that defines the character, a payoff that satisfies. One critical path plus max two branches; lock theme integration into that path. Volume over perfection; placeholders for names; top-down polish — most polish on the first third, where most players engage. The three-fix rule: blocking bugs, confusing choices, immersion breaks. "The story works in my tool" does not equal "the export works." "Good enough ships" — a complete fifteen-minute story beats an unfinished masterpiece. Lock content two hours before deadline. Browser instant-play gets significantly higher engagement.
