Full report is in your Drive. The jam feel findings. The feel problem under a jam clock: game feel is built one tiny decision at a time. Jan Willem Nijman demonstrated this live in "The Art of Screenshake," applying around thirty small changes — bigger bullets, muzzle flash, hitstop, camera kick — to a deliberately boring shooter until it felt incredible. Feel can't be rushed, so the freeze discipline is: reserve the last six to twelve hours for polish and fluff only; implement zero major features on the final day; aim to have the game finished by Saturday night leaving Sunday for polish. Steve Swink's framework: game feel rests on five components — input response, visual feedback, audio feedback, camera behavior, animation timing. For a three-versus-three grid tactics game, real-time control is minimal by design, so the feel burden lands almost entirely on anticipation plus resolution feedback — telegraphs, hitstop, shake, floaters, barks. And the differentiator argument from two-time LD winner Chevy Ray: "Add visual flair. Barely anybody does this, so ninety-five percent of jam games tend to look like hard, dull prototypes… when an enemy dies, make it EXPLODE into particles everywhere, make buttons and interface elements pulse and move… Make everything alive." Validation — the final feel checklist with specific numbers. From the GameCodex polish and juice checklist: the two-of-three pillars rule — every player action needs feedback from at least two of visual, audio, camera; priority order is screen shake, hitstop, particles, SFX, squash and stretch. Trauma-based shake: trauma zero to one, screen offset equals trauma squared, linear decay; light hit zero-point-one-five to zero-point-two-five at five per second; heavy zero-point-four to zero-point-six at three per second; explosion zero-point-seven to one at one-point-five per second; always offer reduce and disable for accessibility. Hitstop: light two frames, heavy four to five, boss five to eight, death freeze five to ten; keep input buffering and rendering running during hitstop. White flash on hit, one to two frames — called "the single most impactful visual juice technique." Particles: three to eight burst per impact, directional spread, fade out never pop out, pooled and recycled. Squash and stretch: plus-minus ten to twenty percent; always ease, never linear. UI: floating text zero-point-five to zero-point-eight seconds; transitions zero-point-three to zero-point-five seconds, no naked cuts. Audio: pitch variation plus-minus five to fifteen percent, layered SFX, ducking; "Audio is fifty percent of game feel and the most underinvested area." Kill slow-mo: zero-point-one to zero-point-two seconds at zero-point-three timescale.
