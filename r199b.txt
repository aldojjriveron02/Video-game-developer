Part 2 of 3. Real-device workflow: Android over chrome-inspect, iOS over Safari's Develop menu with a Mac. The combined checklist: cool the device, cable to the Mac, remote timeline, five-minute M3 soak, watch the frame graph after the thirty-second throttle onset, record P10 frame time — not just the average. The techniques — last-mile, no pipeline changes. NixieFX checklist: cap DPR at about two — the single biggest mobile win; draw calls in the low hundreds; bake sprites into atlases; treat hot-path allocation as a correctness bug — zero steady-state allocation after warmup, no map or filter per frame, ring buffers instead of shift, never build strings every frame; pools that are BOUND, prewarmed to realistic peak, overwrite every field on acquire, and define cap behavior — drop the newest low-value effect when exhausted; watch transparent overdraw; audit UI hot paths like HP bars. MDN canvas: pre-render repeated sprites offscreen; integer coordinates; never scale inside drawImage; layer multiple canvases instead of redrawing everything; alpha-false on the context; batch draws; avoid shadowBlur whenever possible; avoid per-frame text; rAF never setInterval. Fixed timestep decouples sim cost from render cost so a heavy frame degrades smoothly. What to avoid. Last-minute additions — Losh's "bad" list is headed by deadline features; every unprofiled addition in the final hours is a budget risk. Optimizing the wrong thing — don't cut visible quality to hit a synthetic number; measure render passes separately first. Unvalidated deploy environment — the itch build is a different runtime than dev; test the deployed build on a real device, cooled, power-saving off.
