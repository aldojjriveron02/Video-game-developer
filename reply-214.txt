The jam feel findings. Jamzo's core thesis: "on a platform where voters play your entry for a few minutes and move on, juice is often what makes a simple mechanic memorable instead of forgettable." Every effect is a feedback signal confirming input landed; feel makes the game legible and rewarding — "a gray box with great feel beats a beautiful sprite that moves like a spreadsheet cell." LD's own rules: "The ideal LD game can be played right in the browser, in a few minutes, alone" — so the M3 VEX duel slice is exactly the right packaging shape. The start screen is the first thing voters touch: a button that scales and ticks on hover and depresses on click buys goodwill before gameplay starts. UI juice is mandatory, not garnish.

Validation — the agnostic protocol specifics. Nielsen: test five users per qualitative study; better to distribute budget across more studies than more users per study — "ROI drops like a stone with a bigger N"; for low-overhead agile, "often optimal to test as few as two users per study." NNGroup's cheat sheet: "Avoid influencing participants during testing" — facilitators too often jump in. Shafer's blind protocol: run unguided; observation is the key data source; have players think aloud; do NOT coach, explain, or reword rules; note where they stall — that is the finding. Schade on leading questions: the interviewer is "the authority in the room" — participants confirm what they think you want. Neutral only: "What are you trying to do?" and "What just happened?" — never "Did the hit-stop feel good?" The execution shape: two rounds of five blind testers on the M3 duel, iPhone-class touch plus desktop, silent observation plus think-aloud, one task — "beat the VEX duel" — success equals completes unassisted; log every stall over five seconds, reusing our existing stall-hint timer as the threshold. Recruit outside the dev's circle — friends and family confirm biases, not findings.

The techniques — with numbers. Hit-stop: Jamzo says freeze "for a tiny moment when a big hit lands, around point-oh-five seconds"; used on every minor event it reads as lag. GameDesign.gg: freeze a few frames at impact; Smash Bros. scales hitlag with damage — matching our rarity ladder; warns multi-hit attacks need care or combat turns gluey. Our hit-stop rate cap is exactly the right guard: map thirty-to-eighty-millisecond tiers to the rarity ladder, never global. Screen shake: Jamzo — short and sharp, decay quickly — "a shake should be a punch, not an earthquake"; explicitly an accessibility consideration with a player-facing toggle. GameDesign.gg: "the loudest tool in the juice toolbox, potent in small doses"; scale shake to meaning — "a pistol and a rocket must not" shake the same; "screen shake is punctuation. An exclamation point per sentence is emphasis." Ship intensity toggles. Particles: a handful at the moment of an event — dust on land, sparks on impact — cheap, aims the eye. Our pool is prewarmed and FROZEN — correct architecture for iOS. Easing: run slides through ease-in-out, anticipation before action plus overshoot before settling; squash on landing, stretch at speed; keep direct player movement snappy — easing belongs to non-input motion. Audio: a sound on every meaningful action with pitch varied slightly each time so repetition doesn't grate — our WebAudio synth plus limiter plus ducking already implements this. The Vlambeer reference: Nijman's "Art of Screenshake" stacked about thirty tiny tweaks onto a deliberately flat prototype — almost all the gain was feedback, not content.

What to avoid. One: over-juice into unreadable — constant heavy shake makes people queasy and hides the action; five-second particle bursts turn the screen into confetti; hit-stop too long reads as the game freezing; easing on everything makes controls mushy. The test: if an effect makes it harder to see or react, dial it back. Two: post-Vlambeer shake overdose — "indie games shipped with seasickness-grade defaults." Keep our default conservative. Three: new feel features after freeze — LD mandates bug-fix-only plus changelog after submission hour. Four: audio autoplay on iOS — re-verify the WebAudio unlock path in the LD package build, not just dev. Five: friends-only playtesting plus leading questions — "Do you like the feel?" produces compliments, not data. Six: tech risk in final hours — juice can't rescue an unfun core; don't spend the last six hours on particles instead of duel legibility.
