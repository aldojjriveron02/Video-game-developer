The jam save findings — and the big one first. itch.io's iframe sandbox. Admin leafo: itch must run games in an iframe on a different domain for security — untrusted JS — plus site-locking against aggregators that steal games to put ads on top. The consequence: when the player blocks third-party storage — Chrome with third-party cookies blocked, or Safari on iPhone with default "Prevent cross-site tracking" ON — localStorage is denied or silently degraded in the embed, even though it works on localhost. The Fracas 3 devlog: "if you block third-party cookies in Chrome, HTML5 games hosted on itch.io are denied access to LocalStorage and SessionStorage, even though it still works if you're testing using localhost." The itch forum thread: users "lost hours of progress." This is why Export-primary is the correct posture — and on iPhone 17, the storage-degraded path is not an edge case, it's the mainstream path. The Survival Guide budgets four of forty-eight hours for publish and submit and warns "you definitely don't want to be looking for an exporter during Submission Hour." MDN documents navigator.storage.persist — one line, may reduce eviction.

Validation. bugnet: validate save data integrity BEFORE persisting so corrupt data is never written; never let corrupt data overwrite a good backup; on load, validate before using — checksums distinguish corruption from tampering; backups must verify before use. Migration: every save carries a version; chain stepwise v1 to v2 to v3, then validate AFTER migration. Quota: wrap everything in try-catch; handle QuotaExceededError and SecurityError plus private-browsing blocks; five-meg cap. Test matrix: saves from multiple previous versions, all upgrade paths, mid-save crashes, incomplete saves, log every failure. And the probe lesson: leafo recommends try-catch detection, but a dev found Safari can PARTIALLY fail storage calls in ways try-catch misses. The robust pattern is a probe — write a test key, read it back, verify equality, delete it. Our probeStorage already does exactly this.

The techniques. Atomic writes: write to a temp key, verify, then swap — a crash mid-write leaves old data intact. Rolling backups: three to five rotating slots; on corruption, restore the most recent good. At our save size — well under fifty kilobytes — each save costs under a millisecond, so there's no perf excuse to skip backups. Throttled saves: dirty flag, at most once per second, never inside the frame loop — five hundred kilobytes costs five to ten milliseconds on desktop, worse on mobile, and our twelve-millisecond budget can't take a mid-combat hitch. Save on visibilitychange plus the Page Lifecycle API, not just beforeunload — iOS Safari beforeunload is unreliable. Export-import: JSON to base64 string, shareable and downloadable; import with full validation after migration.

What to avoid. Testing saves only on localhost — the itch iframe behaves differently; everything works in dev, saves silently die for judges. Assuming try-catch detection is enough — Safari partial-fails. iOS defaults — tracking prevention is ON by default; on our target device this is mainstream. Saving in the frame loop. beforeunload-only. Overwriting the only good backup with corrupt data. Trusting browser durability. Discovering the export pipeline during Submission Hour.
