Full report is in your Drive. The jam tutorial findings. Thompson's rater problem: LD games are rated on Overall, Innovation, Fun, Theme, Graphics, Audio, Humor — raters decide fast, and a tutorial that delays "the fun" directly costs Fun and Overall points. His rules: submit a warmup prototype before the jam so the pipeline is proven — we satisfy this; set a personal deadline two hours before the real one because submission hour is when things go most wrong; don't ship missing categories — his LD28 had no audio, automatic N/A. George Fan's attention-budget problem from his GDC twenty-twelve talk: players arrive with a FINITE willingness to learn; it grows only as they play. His two rules: "Use Fewer Words" and "Just get the player to do it once." A jam tutorial must spend that budget only on what is unique. Hedges' player interviews: tutorials players LIKE don't feel like tutorials, don't disrupt flow, have low fail chance, include a tutorial boss, are skippable, allow experimentation, use short messages, focus on unique mechanics. Dark Souls' Undead Asylum is the model: early failure nearly impossible, free experimentation, then a tutorial boss. Mega Man X's first level taught everything with no tutorial section at all. Validation — Kleenex execution specifics. Eastshade's tissue testing: watch someone who has NEVER played, with ZERO explaining or guiding — "the more unfamiliar they are with your game and the less you speak while they play it, the better." Called tissue because a player can only be first-time once — be frugal with fresh eyes. One to two testers per session generated a month of fixes.
