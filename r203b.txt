Part 2 of 3. The audit workbook shape: thirty-six manual checks across legibility, input, captions and motion, settings, and release regressions, each logged Pass, Needs review, or Not tested. And AbleGamers' APX: twenty-two design patterns, twelve Access plus ten Challenge, with the jam triage rule — "If you have limited time, budget, and resources, and cannot address all accessibility issues, then Access should be your priority." Design for barriers, not disabilities. The techniques — GAG Basic, the jam tier. High-contrast text and UI. Simple clear formatting. Never convey essential info by color alone. Large well-spaced touch targets. Separate volume controls for effects, speech, and music. Subtitles for important speech. No essential info by sound alone. Wide difficulty choice including adjust-during-play. All settings saved and remembered. Game speed option. Skip and practice options. Interactive tutorials. Progress at your own pace through text. Color: pair every color-coded element with a shape, icon, or pattern from the first mockup; test against all three CVD simulators; verify contrast across real in-game backgrounds — bright, dark, bloom, particles — not just menus; WCAG four-point-five to one for text, three to one for UI. Subtitles: build into the dialogue pipeline; dialogue and non-speech captions as distinct types; scale to two hundred percent; speaker identification with direction; on by default. Input: semantic actions not physical keys; prompts that read the current binding; alternate presets; configurable hold-or-toggle. And Celeste's Assist Mode as GAG's own best-practice example — granular variables, game speed, assists, with unpatronising wording. What to avoid. Hamilton's cardinal trap: "the biggest mistake developers can make is not stepping out of their own shoes." The entrenched misconception: that accessibility is difficult, expensive, and only benefits a few. The bolted-on retrofit: colorblind palettes fighting particle systems never designed for them; remapping breaking minigames; subtitles forced into a UI that can't hold them.
