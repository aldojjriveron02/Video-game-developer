Full report is in your Drive. The jam a11y findings. Scott and Ghinea's Global Game Jam study: organizers injected an accessibility challenge into a jam with thirty-five jammers; thirty-one answered the questionnaire with strong interest — evidence that a light, explicit a11y challenge inside a jam is "a productive way to promote accessibility awareness." The jam problem in one line: forty-eight to seventy-two-hour builds ship whatever is cheapest, so accessibility only lands if it is scaffolded and FROZEN, not invented, in the final hours. The scaffold model ships: Global Game Jam has run accessibility diversifiers repeatedly — GGJ18 had "One tap for all," "I can see clearly now" with text-to-background contrast at four-point-five to one, "Basics covered" addressing the top-four most-complained-about issues, "Over to you" for configurable speed and size, "Let me see" for customisable colours; GGJ 2025 had "Easy-read"; GGJ 2026 has "Hands Off." The four most-complained-about issues, per Ian Hamilton's observations: fixed control schemes, text size, color reliance, subtitle presentation. Validation — the freeze checklist with numbers. Game Accessibility Guidelines, Basic tier: thirty checklist items. Flicker and photosensitivity: avoid anything flashing MORE THAN three times per second covering at least twenty-five percent of the screen; avoid flashing sequences longer than five seconds without a skip; never use the term "epilepsy safe" — no test proves it. Text: Amazon's ten-foot UI guidelines recommend a minimum twenty-eight-pixel font; best-practice examples cited are Guacamelee and Lovers in a Dangerous Spacetime. Contrast: aim for WCAG two AA compliant in the Snook checker — four-point-five to one for normal text.
