The theme exists to solve the "blank canvas" problem — it gives thousands of jammers a shared creative constraint so they can start immediately. Theme use is NOT required by the rules — it is a recommendation, and since LD50 participants can opt out of the Theme rating category. BUT if you stay opted in, raters judge the fit. Theme is one of seven official rating categories: Fun, Innovation, Theme, Graphics, Audio, Humor, Mood. Community members rate entries during a two-to-three-week voting window; no panel of judges. The core tension for our deploy-ready hangar: the hangar is the game's proven core — it must NOT be redesigned at six PM Friday. Theme integration must therefore be a content, framing, and narrative layer over a stable core, not a mechanics rewrite. Raters score what they can perceive: "there should be a clear link between your game and the theme, so an untrained player can encounter the game cold." A theme only visible in the devlog does not score. Validation — THEME_HOOK execution specifics. One: verb matching, the engineer's technique — brainstorm the theme's VERBS, then route those verbs through the hook into existing mechanics and labels. Koster's theory of fun underpins this: theme works when the player experiences the same verbs in mechanics. Two: three matching layers the hook can drive without touching core sim — narrative matching, a one-paragraph story with hangar and combat labels renamed to the theme's verbs; aesthetic matching, adjectives of the theme guiding UI tint, pilot barks, mission briefings; tension and stakes, theme as conflict between opposing aspects, Sid Meier's "interesting choices." Three: unexpected angle beats literal read — "Just look at theme from angle no one would." Examples: "Assemble" became blind chemical-element assembly; "Odd One Out" became spot-the-AI chat messages; "Defense plus time" became cybersecurity.
