Part 2 of 3. The techniques — last-mile polish. Vois's bark checklist: write variants around trigger frequency and gameplay purpose; separate informational from personality; per-trigger audit of frequency, urgency, overlap, competing sounds; three variants for routine as a starting exercise; actionable info near the beginning; duration budget per trigger; test the line while doing the action; every line gets a specific intention; stable IDs; approve a small batch first. "Write the line the player needs, then give it permission to stay quiet." Beaulieu on bark craft: a bark's first function is to inform; two to ten variations per trigger; spread lore through universe-specific language like Orwell's Newspeak so even uncomprehended terms build immersion; characterize beyond faction — how does a shy versus annoyed character say hello; "narrative potential" — turn a bark into a dramatic question so the player fills gaps; a repeated bark can become a Proust's madeleine, like Age of Empires villagers — a gimmick can be a signature. His constraint ethic — "telling one or several information in only a few words" — is directly consonant with our eight-word rule. What to avoid. Text walls before first input — violates the LD ideal and judges' reality. Repetition grating — Skyrim's "Never should have come here"; per Vois, "more lines don't fix excessive triggering," fix cooldowns and priority.
