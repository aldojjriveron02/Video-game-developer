The jam a11y findings. itch.io's Accessibility Jam from twenty-twenty-one: rated entries for accessibility in eight categories — five disability-group-based, three hardware-based — plus different gameplay options, clear instructions, and overall game. Accessibility was ninety percent of the rating. The core asks: clear instructions — controls, goal, how the game ends — in the game itself or at the top of the game description; browser play matters because many don't want to download anything; customizability scores. HyperDot's prioritization heuristic from solo dev Charles McGregor: choose changelog items by "highest impact for most players and what I could feasibly implement" — a usable triage rule for jam week. And the itch.io game page is part of the submission's accessibility surface: document features on the page, not just in-game. Validation — the maintenance protocol. abratabia's six-step: one, automated audits with Lighthouse, axe, WAVE against WCAG two-point-one AA; two, keyboard-only playtest — unplug the mouse; three, screen reader testing with NVDA, VoiceOver, TalkBack; four, colorblind simulation; five, two-hundred-percent zoom playthrough plus fully-muted-audio playthrough; six, playtesting with disabled players via AbleGamers panels and others. The maintenance clause: repeat "after every update or new release." GAG advanced tier: "Solicit accessibility feedback" — make it easy, keep a record — and "Ensure that all settings are saved and remembered" — persistence across loads and updates is a validation item.
