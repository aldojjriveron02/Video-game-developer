Part 2 of 3. Quota: wrap everything in try-catch; handle QuotaExceededError and SecurityError plus private-browsing blocks; five-meg cap. Test matrix: saves from multiple previous versions, all upgrade paths, mid-save crashes, incomplete saves, log every failure. And the probe lesson: leafo recommends try-catch detection, but a dev found Safari can PARTIALLY fail storage calls in ways try-catch misses. The robust pattern is a probe — write a test key, read it back, verify equality, delete it. Our probeStorage already does exactly this. The techniques. Atomic writes: write to a temp key, verify, then swap — a crash mid-write leaves old data intact. Rolling backups: three to five rotating slots; on corruption, restore the most recent good. At our save size — well under fifty kilobytes — each save costs under a millisecond, so there's no perf excuse to skip backups. Throttled saves: dirty flag, at most once per second, never inside the frame loop — five hundred kilobytes costs five to ten milliseconds on desktop, worse on mobile, and our twelve-millisecond budget can't take a mid-combat hitch. Save on visibilitychange plus the Page Lifecycle API, not just beforeunload — iOS Safari beforeunload is unreliable. Export-import: JSON to base64 string, shareable and downloadable; import with full validation after migration. What to avoid. Testing saves only on localhost — the itch iframe behaves differently; everything works in dev, saves silently die for judges. Assuming try-catch detection is enough — Safari partial-fails. iOS defaults — tracking prevention is ON by default; on our target device this is mainstream.
