LD judges score narrative directly: the official rules define MOOD as "Storytelling, emotion, and the vibe you get while playing" and HUMOR as "How amusing a game is. Humorous dialog, funny sounds." Our pilot barks sit exactly on the Humor category; our Mood targeting maps to the Mood category. This is a real competitive lever, not garnish. The ideal LD game "can be played right in the browser, in a few minutes, alone" — the submission-page narrative must get a judge from page-load to first input in under two minutes, with zero lore prerequisite. The submission page is the first narrative act: "people don't like to read" — page narrative must be visual and terse; the cited best example is Doki Doki Literature Club, which "brings its game world to its game page." GameDeveloper's three phases: preparation, execution, networking — finish two hours before the deadline; post-jam, rate about ten games a day. NarraScope's jam sticky: mechanics and hook FIRST, then write narrative to emphasize the mechanic; keep narrative under five thousand words for a two-week jam — proportionally far less for seventy-two hours. Validation — the three-player one-shot specifics. Sample math: Nielsen's five users find eighty-five percent; MeasuringU's p of point-three-one. With three players, expected discovery is one minus point-six-nine cubed — about sixty-five percent. A defensible one-shot gate for a slice, but Koji says run two to three small rounds instead of one big one if a second evening exists.
